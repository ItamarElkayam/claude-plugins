  A defensible gap statement has three parts: (a) what is missing, (b) the evidence that it
  is missing *from this corpus* — the claim ids that circumscribe what has been done, and
  (c) why it matters. Distinguish:

  - **untested conditions** — a method exists but was never evaluated in a regime the corpus
    itself flags as important (name the regime and cite where it is flagged);
  - **unreplicated results** — a consequential finding rests on one study, one dataset, or
    one author group;
  - **measurement gaps** — the outcome that matters is never measured, or is measured with
    incomparable definitions across papers;
  - **contradictions left unresolved** — cite the conflicting claim ids and state which
    experiment would settle it;
  - **method-transfer gaps** — a mechanism proven for task A has never been applied to task B
    (say plainly that this is an opportunity, not a finding).

  Never present a gap as a finding, and never claim a gap exists in the *field* when your
  evidence only shows it is absent from *this corpus*. Say "not covered by this corpus".
